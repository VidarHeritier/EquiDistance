    Spherical Equidistance & Trilateration Math

    How points equidistant from multiple locations are computed:

    • 2 Cities: The set of all equidistant points forms the spherical perpendicular bisector (a great circle). For a chosen distance D, the circles of radius D intersect at 0, 1, or 2 antipodal points.
    • 3 Cities (e.g. Shanghai, New York, São Paulo): There exist exactly two spherical circumcenters on Earth (antipodal poles) equidistant to all three cities simultaneously!
    • 4 to 10 Cities: Circles generally do not intersect at a single mathematical point. The explorer calculates pairwise small-circle intersections and computes least-squares spherical variance to find the true geographic convergence zones.

    Distance on Sphere: d = R · arccos(c₁ · c₂) (Earth Radius R ≈ 6,371 km)
